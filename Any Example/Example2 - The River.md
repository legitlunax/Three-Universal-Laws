# Example 2: The River

A river is a useful image for the three universal laws.

## 1. Chaos

At the source, the river is not a river yet in the strict sense. It is a collection of rain, runoff, springs, and broken paths. It is fluid, unbounded, and moving in many directions.

This is chaos:

- many streams
- no fixed direction
- no single purpose yet
- endless potential

The world is not organized; it is becoming.

## 2. Order

The river follows gravity, terrain, and the shape of the land. It gathers into a channel. It forms a flow with rhythm and structure.

This is order:

- the water finds a path
- the valley gives it shape
- many small flows become one meaningful current

Order does not destroy possibility. It organizes possibility into movement.

## 3. Singularity

At the river's deepest point, it becomes singular in its role: it carries water toward the sea. Not many purposes, but one clear function.

This is singularity:

- one direction
- one destination
- one final purpose

The river is no longer scattered. It is focused.

## The lesson

Life often begins in chaos, becomes ordered through experience, and then arrives at a more exact purpose.

The three laws are not enemies. They are stages of becoming:

- Chaos is potential.
- Order is structure.
- Singularity is focus.

A river is not one thing only. It is a process of becoming clear.
