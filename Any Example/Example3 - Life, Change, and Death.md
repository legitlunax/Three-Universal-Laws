# Example 3: Life, Change, and Death

The three universal laws can also describe a cycle of becoming: what we become, how we become it, and how we return to simplicity.

## 1. Chaos: Life — What We Become

Life begins with possibility. A living being grows into a unique form through countless experiences, relationships, choices, and circumstances.

Chaos represents the many things we may become:

- different identities and roles
- unplanned experiences
- competing desires and possibilities

In this sense, chaos is not simply disorder. It is the richness and uncertainty of being alive.

## 2. Order: Change — How We Become

Change is the process that shapes possibility into experience. Some changes are chosen; others come from time, relationships, nature, or events beyond our control.

Order represents how becoming takes shape:

- experiences build on one another
- habits and decisions create patterns
- patterns shape who we are

Order does not mean that life is unchanging. It is the structure through which change happens.

## 3. Singularity: Death — Returning to Simplicity

Death can be understood here as the end of a particular life and its many possibilities. A complex story reaches its limit; the individual form returns to simplicity.

Singularity represents that final focus:

- one life, no longer unfolding into new possibilities
- a distinct story brought to an end
- a return from complexity to stillness

This is a philosophical metaphor, not a claim about what happens after death. It treats death as the boundary that gives a life its particular shape.

## The Cycle

Through this lens:

- **Chaos is life:** the many things we may become.
- **Order is change:** the process by which we become.
- **Singularity is death:** the return of one particular life to simplicity.

The three laws describe different parts of one movement: possibility opens, experience shapes, and a finite form comes to rest.
