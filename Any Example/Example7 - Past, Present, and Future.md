# Example 7: Past, Present, and Future

The three laws can also be used as a lens for time. This is a way to think about how history, action, and possibility relate—not a proof that the future is predetermined.

## 1. Chaos: The Future as Possibility

Before an event happens, more than one outcome may be possible. Some possibilities are likely, some unlikely, and some may be ruled out by what has already happened.

Chaos represents this open field:

- possible choices and consequences
- events not yet resolved
- uncertainty about which path will occur

The future is not necessarily unlimited: physical conditions, available information, and earlier events constrain what can happen.

## 2. Order: The Present as Change

The present is where conditions and actions interact. A person makes a choice, a system processes an input, or nature continues through its current state.

Order represents how the present turns possibility into change:

- current conditions shape available actions
- rules and relationships influence outcomes
- an event moves the world from one state to another

The present is not a motionless point in this analogy; it is the ongoing process of becoming.

## 3. Singularity: The Past as a Settled History

Once an event has happened, that particular event becomes part of history. Its other possible outcomes are no longer the outcome that occurred.

Singularity represents the way a moment becomes specific:

- one choice was made
- one result entered the record
- the event became part of the past

The past is not always perfectly known. Memories, logs, and historical accounts are records of what happened, but each record can be incomplete or mistaken.

## Destiny and the Cycle

In this view, destiny is not necessarily a fixed script. It can mean the set of futures made more or less possible by the past, together with the actions taken in the present.

```text
past: settled events and inherited conditions
  -> present: choices and processes that change the state
  -> future: possibilities constrained, but not always uniquely determined
```

- **Chaos:** what may happen.
- **Order:** how events and choices shape what happens.
- **Singularity:** the specific outcome that becomes history.

As the future becomes present and then past, the cycle continues. What happened constrains what can happen next, while present actions still matter.
